## Where AI actually helps in retail and e-commerce

Swiss online retail keeps growing. According to Handelsverband.swiss, online consumption reached CHF 15.8 billion in 2025, up 6 percent, with CHF 13 billion spent at Swiss providers. Growth means more orders, more questions about delivery and returns, and more product data to keep consistent, usually without proportionally more staff.

Customer service is the obvious place for AI, but the evidence on customer attitudes is mixed. A Gartner survey of 3,566 B2B and B2C customers, conducted in early 2026, found that 87 percent say companies using generative AI for service must offer access to a human agent. The same coverage reports that service leaders put a median 12 percent of their 2025 budget into AI while only 24 percent reported positive financial returns. The lesson is to automate the routine and make the exit to a person easy.

Returns are another real cost. Swiss Post notes that in fashion the return rate is often very high, and cites research that about 40 percent of returns are due to damaged goods, while only every second online shop asks why items come back. Better product information and tracked return reasons are tasks an agent can support.

## What we build

### Order and returns assistant
A chatbot that answers where an order is, starts a return, and explains your policy, connected to your shop and carrier systems. Refunds above a threshold, complaints and anything unusual go to a staff member with the full conversation.

### Product advice assistant
A chatbot that answers sizing, compatibility and usage questions from your catalogue and product data, in German, French, Italian and English. It only states what is in your data and says so when it does not know.

### Catalogue enrichment agent
An agent that drafts product descriptions, attributes and translations from supplier data, flags missing or inconsistent fields, and queues the result for an editor to approve before publishing.

### Returns insights agent
An agent that categorises free-text return reasons and customer messages, so merchandising and content teams can see which product pages cause avoidable returns.

### Cloud setup for shop integrations
Hosting and integrations in Switzerland or the EU that connect the assistants to your shop, ERP and helpdesk, with monitoring and cost limits.

## Compliance and data considerations

Retail chatbots handle names, addresses, order histories and sometimes payment queries. The Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection (nFADP/revDSG) applies directly to AI-supported processing, according to the FDPIC. Operators must be transparent about purpose and data sources, let people know they are dealing with a machine, and allow automated individual decisions to be reviewed by a person. If you sell into the EU, GDPR also applies.

The EU AI Act applies in stages. Most retail assistants are unlikely to fall into its high-risk categories, but we would check each use case, and we would avoid anything touching credit or eligibility decisions.

In practice we design for clear AI disclosure at the start of every chat, a visible route to a human at all times, Swiss or EU data residency, retention limits on conversation logs, and no payment card data in the chat. We also keep logs so you can review failures and correct answers.

## How an engagement starts

We start by looking at your actual support tickets to find the questions that repeat, then pilot one channel with a small share of traffic. We measure resolution, handovers and customer feedback against your current baseline. If it does not help, we will say so, and if it does, we widen the scope gradually.
