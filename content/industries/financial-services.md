## Where AI actually helps in financial services

Banks and insurers run on documents and rules: onboarding files, KYC checks, claims forms, policy questions, regulatory reporting. Much of that work is repetitive, but it sits inside tightly supervised processes, so the question is rarely whether AI can do a task. It is whether you can show who is accountable for the result.

Swiss institutions are already moving. In its 2025 survey of about 400 supervised institutions, FINMA found that roughly half use AI or have applications in development, and another 25 percent plan to within three years. Of those using AI, 91 percent also use generative AI. FINMA also reported that only about half of institutions have a formal AI strategy within their governance framework, and that smaller firms often depend entirely on third-party applications.

That gap is where a small, well-scoped project can help. The work that pays off first is usually internal and bounded: answering staff and customer questions from approved documents, preparing case files for a human reviewer, and routing incoming requests. Autonomous decisions about credit or pricing are a different matter, and we would advise against starting there.

## What we build

### Policy and product assistant
A chatbot that answers customer or advisor questions strictly from your approved policy wording, product sheets and FAQs, with a citation to the source passage. When it cannot find an answer, it hands the conversation to a person with the context attached.

### Claims and onboarding intake agent
An agent that reads incoming claims or onboarding documents, extracts the fields, checks them for completeness against your rules, and drafts a case summary. A case handler reviews and approves before anything is booked.

### KYC and compliance document preparation
An agent that collects the evidence for periodic reviews from your internal systems and assembles a draft file with open points flagged. It does not make the risk decision. It saves the analyst the gathering and formatting.

### Internal knowledge search for regulation and procedures
A private search and Q&A tool over your handbooks, circulars and process descriptions, so staff find the current rule quickly and see which document it came from.

### Cloud environment for regulated workloads
Infrastructure in Swiss or EU regions with access controls, logging and a documented inventory of models, data flows and data sources, so the AI side is easy to audit.

## Compliance and data considerations

FINMA Guidance 08/2024, published on 18 December 2024, sets out expectations for governance and risk management when supervised institutions use AI. It recommends a detailed inventory of tools, data flows and data sources, attention to data quality and testing, and notes the growing dependence on third parties such as cloud and model providers. Its central principle is that responsibility for decisions cannot be delegated to AI or to third parties. We design for that: every agent has a named business owner, and consequential steps need human approval.

The revised Federal Act on Data Protection (nFADP/revDSG), in force since 1 September 2023, applies directly to AI-supported processing. According to the FDPIC, operators must make the purpose, functionality and data sources transparent, tell people when they are dealing with a machine, and honour the right to have automated individual decisions reviewed by a person.

For work with EU customers, the EU AI Act matters. Annex III lists AI used to evaluate the creditworthiness of natural persons, and AI used for risk assessment and pricing in life and health insurance, as high-risk. Under the current implementation timeline, Annex III high-risk requirements apply from 2 December 2027. Switzerland has no AI Act of its own. The Federal Council has chosen a sector-specific approach and intends to ratify the Council of Europe AI Convention.

In practice we propose Swiss or EU hosting, full audit logs of prompts, sources and outputs, role-based access, and human approval on anything that affects a customer's money or coverage. Your compliance team should confirm the final setup. We are not a law firm.

## How an engagement starts

We begin with a short scoping conversation and, if there is a fit, a small pilot on one process, with a baseline we measure together beforehand. If the pilot does not clearly help, we say so. If it does, we expand step by step, and FINMA expects early contact for AI in critical processes, so we would involve your compliance function from the start.
