## Where AI actually helps in legal and professional services

Law firms, fiduciaries and consultancies sell expertise but spend much of their time on reading and sorting: contracts, correspondence, case files, intake emails. These tasks are well suited to AI-assisted drafting and summarising, as long as a qualified person stays responsible for the result.

The constraint is confidentiality. The Swiss Bar Association (SAV/FSA) adopted guidelines on the use of AI in June 2024, with a consolidated version dated 16 February 2025. According to a summary of those guidelines, Art. 13 BGFA and Art. 321 StGB apply equally to AI services, and providers with access to client data are treated like auxiliaries under professional secrecy. Many public AI tools simply do not meet that bar.

The guidelines also say the lawyer remains responsible and AI outputs must be verified for substance and accuracy. In other words, the realistic goal is faster first drafts and better organised files, not unsupervised legal advice.

## What we build

### Private document review assistant
An assistant that reads a set of contracts or case documents inside an environment you control, extracts key clauses, dates and parties, and produces a summary with links to the source passages. The lawyer checks each point against the original.

### Internal knowledge search
A search and question-answering tool over your own precedents, templates and past memos. Answers always cite the documents they come from, so the professional can verify before relying on them.

### Client intake chatbot
A chatbot on your website that collects the basic facts of a new matter, checks for conflicts of interest against your client list and books an initial call. It gives no legal advice and says so clearly. A person reviews every intake.

### Time entry and file drafting
An agent that drafts time entries from emails and calendar items, and prepares file notes and routine letters from templates. Fee earners review and approve before anything is billed or sent.

### Private cloud deployment
We set up hosting and access control so the whole system runs in a Swiss or EU environment under your control, with every action logged.

## Compliance and data considerations

For lawyers, professional secrecy (Anwaltsgeheimnis, Art. 321 StGB and Art. 13 BGFA) is the central issue. According to the SAV guidelines as summarised by CASUS, firms using third-party AI tools should have written data processing agreements under the revised Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection, hosting in Switzerland or the EU, zero data retention and no training on client information. The guidelines also recommend telling clients when AI is materially involved, though that is not a strict statutory requirement. Fiduciaries and other professions with secrecy duties should take a similar approach.

The FDPIC states that the Swiss data protection act, in force since 1 September 2023, applies directly to AI-supported processing and asks for transparency about purpose and data sources. For EU clients, the GDPR applies too. Chatbots that talk to the public must disclose that they are AI under Article 50 of the EU AI Act, which has applied since 2 August 2026.

Our design follows from this: Swiss or EU hosting, no use of your data to train models, role-based access per matter, full audit logs, source citations in every answer and a human sign-off step before anything reaches a client or a court. Your own professional rules decide in the end, so we would work with your compliance lead or managing partner from the start.
